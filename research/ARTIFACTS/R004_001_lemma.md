# Lemma L1 — prime-support uniformity for A^x + B^y = C^z (R004 artifact)

## Statement
Let A, B, C, x, y, z be positive integers with A^x + B^y = C^z.
Let p be prime. Then p divides at least two of {A, B, C} if and only if p
divides all three.

## Proof
(⇐) is trivial. (⇒) in three cases:
1. p | A and p | B: then p | (A^x + B^y) = C^z, so p | C (a prime divisor of a
   perfect power divides its base).
2. p | A and p | C: then p | (C^z − A^x) = B^y, so p | B.
3. p | B and p | C: then p | (C^z − B^y) = A^x, so p | A. ∎

No condition on exponents beyond positivity is used; Beal's x,y,z > 2 is not
needed for the lemma.

## Corollary (modular obstruction)
If gcd(A,B,C) = 1 but (A,B,C) is not pairwise coprime, then A^x + B^y = C^z is
impossible for every choice of positive exponents x,y,z.
Proof: take p dividing some pair; by L1 it would divide the third,
contradicting triple-coprimality. Equivalently mod p: two terms vanish and the
third does not. ∎

## Scope note
The lemma is elementary (novelty KNOWN). Its value here is compression: it
decides all 1530 class-P lines of beal_near.txt at once. It does not touch
class C (1528 fully-coprime lines) — those need other ideas (see R002/R003).
T = ∅ in this dataset is forced by the hunter's coprime-(A,B) prefilter, not
by this lemma.
